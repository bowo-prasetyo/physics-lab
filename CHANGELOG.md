# Changelog

What Physica has built, and what had to be reworked along the way. The [curriculum roadmap](docs/product/CURRICULUM_ROADMAP.md) lists what comes next; this file is the record of what is done. Counts in an entry (questions, problems, steps) are as they were when the entry was written; the code is the source for today's numbers.

Each entry describes what exists and what it is designed to do. None of it is evidence that learning improved: that needs the [assessment pilot](docs/product/ASSESSMENT_PILOT_PLAN.md).

## Unreleased

- **Every level adds a prediction in Force, mass and acceleration.** The experiment had only two Beginner predictions, so Intermediate and Advanced added maths text but nothing to test. An Intermediate prediction now compares 20 N on 2 kg with 40 N on 4 kg (wrong options: the harder-pushed cart, or the lighter one, accelerates more), and an Advanced one asks how far a cart from rest goes in twice the time (wrong options: twice as far; somewhere between two and four times). The trial table now records the elapsed time and displacement so the Advanced comparison can be read from it. A test now checks that every experiment has a prediction starting at each level; before, it covered only four experiments.
- **Counts out of the vision; delivered work into this changelog.** The product vision repeated how many graph and synthesis questions existed, and the numbers had drifted from the code. They were removed, and the roadmap's *Delivered* section moved here.
- **An outcome map (draft).** [docs/product/OUTCOME_MAP.md](docs/product/OUTCOME_MAP.md) links each learning outcome in the vision to the experiment predictions that test it and the pilot items that assess it, and marks it covered, partially covered or planned. A test checks that every outcome is listed and every named prediction exists.

## Delivered to November 2026

The record kept in the roadmap until it moved here, oldest first.

- **Honest coverage.** The home page separates what implemented experiments explore from planned topics. `src/modules.ts` is the single catalogue; the translated catalogues, the teacher guide's lesson coverage and the README are checked against it, so they cannot drift.
- **One learning loop.** Every experiment uses the shared predict–observe–explain cards. Each level adds its own question, each explanation unlocks only after the run that tests it, and results stay hidden until the learner has run the current setup.
- **One page layout.** Every experiment page has the same sections in the same order (header, share, investigation guide, Predict, Experiment, Mathematics, Meaning, next steps), enforced by a test.
- **Experiments:** Module 3 Force Lab, Momentum and collisions, and Circular motion and orbits; Module 4 length contraction; Module 5 geodesic motion. Circular motion and orbits closes a prerequisite gap: the Geodesic Motion Lab compares curved-spacetime paths with Newtonian ellipses that Module 3 now teaches.
- **Teaching support.** A lesson outline for every experiment, in all three languages, and an anonymous assessment pilot with pre/post, transfer and explanation items for Force Lab and length contraction.
- **Identity.** Module 3 renamed "Understanding motion"; experiment letters and audience labels on levels and modules removed.
- **Learning path.** One path through the thread (`/learning`): chapters are the modules, steps are the experiments, both taken from `src/modules.ts`, with prerequisites for every chapter and step. Concept questions (24, at least one per experiment, at least one harder per module) unlock after the learner has tested a prediction in the experiment, and their options are shuffled.
- **Synthesis questions.** Three questions in Modules 3 and 5 combine models from two experiments: force components with projectile motion, impulse with Newton’s second law, and the moving-clock effect with gravitational clock rates. They are practice questions that `require` the other experiment, so they share the practice data, tests and shuffled options; they name experiments from the catalogue and unlock only after both experiments are explored. Their wrong options are real misconceptions (for example, that a horizontal force changes the flight time, or that the two clock effects cancel in every orbit). A first version kept them in a separate store with hand-copied experiment names, which had already drifted from the catalogue.
- **Work and energy in Module 3, in the experiments.** Force Lab shows the work done by the push, friction, gravity and the normal force in every trial, and that the net work equals the kinetic energy gained; a Beginner prediction asks for the sign of friction's work. Circular motion and orbits shows kinetic and potential energy per kilogram at the closest and farthest points from Intermediate, with a prediction that the total stays the same. Practice covers energy at every level (Beginner: net work at steady speed; Intermediate: energy of a higher orbit; Advanced: two numerical problems), and `coverage-evidence.test.ts` ties each energy topic in the catalogue to the prediction where learners test it. A first version claimed these topics covered on the strength of two Advanced problems alone, with no experiment showing work or energy; it was rebuilt this way.
- **Practice budget.** Every step has at least 2 practice items at Beginner and 3–7 at Advanced, enforced by a test. Length contraction and Force, mass and acceleration were topped up to the minimum.
- **Apply it: numerical problems in the learning path.** The standalone Physics Problem-Solving Lab (`/problem-solving`, now a redirect to `/learning`) was folded into the learning path, because a separate, ungated, formula-first area contradicted *prediction before explanation* and *practice follows the method*. The 30 numerical problems unlock with their step's concept questions; every experiment has a Beginner problem and every module has Intermediate and Advanced ones. Answers within 2% count as correct, and each answer is checked against the physics engine in the tests. Answers remain in page memory only; this is practice, not assessment.
- **One front door.** The home page has a single call to action, *Start the learning path*; the header links to the learning path and the teacher guide only.
- **Graph questions.** A third practice type: questions answered by reading a graph, defined in the learning content so any step can use one. Graphs are position–time runs or model curves computed by the physics engine (a(F) for Force Lab, height against time for the projectile launcher, T(L) for pendulum clocks), on scales that start at zero. *Space versus time* has four questions, from Beginner to Advanced; the other three steps have a Beginner and an Intermediate question each. A first version of the cross-experiment graphs sat outside the steps, was hand-drawn and drew a projectile curve that gravity cannot produce; it was rebuilt on this design.
- **Progressive worked solutions.** Numerical problem solutions now reveal one reasoning step at a time, so learners can pause between model selection, substitution and interpretation instead of seeing the entire worked answer at once. The existing answer checker and numeric diagnosis remain the source of correctness and feedback.
- **House rules and guard tests.** `CLAUDE.md` lists the conventions that changes most often broke. Tests now enforce the main ones: multiple-choice only through the practice data, no hand-drawn graphics in the learning path, no new inline translations (a ratchet over older files), and no English words inside translated accessible labels.
- **Diagnostic feedback on numerical answers.** A wrong answer is told the likely slip: a reversed sign, a power of ten (with the factor and the unit named), rounding (2–5% off) or the model. Marking and feedback share one number parser and one tolerance.
- **Learner experience.** A progress summary across the course; an optional workspace on numerical problems (choose the model, substitute with units, check the result); graph-reading prompts that never show answer values; and an unscored chapter review once a chapter's last step is explored. A first version showed an "average rate" under every graph that answered two of the graph questions and gave a straight-line rate for the curved pendulum graph, and called the review a scored "exam"; both were reworked.
- **Device-local progress (ratified).** Learners may opt in to remembering explored experiments in their own browser, with a visible *Forget progress on this device* control and a shared-device note. Nothing is sent anywhere.
- **Concept bridges, after the evidence.** Eight experiments end their *Meaning* section with a short bridge: the intuition, the common trap and a self-check question. They sit inside *Meaning*, after the run, so they never explain an answer before the learner has tested it; a test keeps every explanation component below the Experiment. Each bridge's equation is typeset and follows the level: Newton's second law, momentum conservation and the clock-rate formulas appear from Beginner, orbital speed and length contraction from Intermediate, and the geodesic equation only at Advanced. A first version rendered the bridges above *Predict*, with every equation, including the geodesic one, at every level, and its Indonesian addressed learners as *kamu*; it was reworked, and a test now keeps Indonesian copy in the *Anda* register.
- **An honest testing checklist.** The pull-request check fails a ticked box that says it was not done, and an unticked box without `Not done: <reason>`; explained not-done lines are shown to the product owner as warnings.
- **Coverage claims are checked against experiments.** Every topic a module lists as covered names the experiment prediction where learners test it, and a test fails on a claim without one. Two older claims, shown in experiment readouts but without a prediction (proper radial distance near a mass; the limits of classical evolution as the scale factor approaches zero), were first listed as exceptions; each now has an Advanced prediction (rulers near a black hole measure 24 % more than the difference in r; the model stops being trustworthy before a reaches 0), and the exception list was removed. A first version of the Module 4–6 additions (velocity addition, relativistic momentum, the equivalence principle and a Hubble diagram) claimed those topics covered on the strength of practice questions alone; they were moved back to planned topics, and their practice was kept.
- **The equivalence principle, in the Geodesic Motion Lab.** After every run, the lab shows two accelerometers: one on the orbiting spacecraft, which reads zero, and one on a spacecraft hovering at the starting radius on its engines, which reads 1/√(1 − rₛ/r) times the Newtonian pull and grows without limit near the horizon. A Beginner prediction asks which reads zero; its wrong options are the misconceptions that a hovering craft "is not moving" and that gravity is "felt" equally at the same distance. The explanation names the principle and its limit: it holds only locally, because tides remain. The hovering reading is computed from the static clock rate (a = c² dα/dr) and checked against it in the tests. The equivalence-principle practice on this step is now backed by the experiment, and the topic is covered.
- **Velocity addition and relativistic momentum, in Space and time versus spacetime.** Two new situations join the headlight, flashes and clock. *Probe*: a ship launches a probe forward at 0.5c; *Momentum*: the ship's own momentum. Each of the three descriptions predicts them by its own route (velocities add; the Lorentz velocity rule or Δτ from the Lorentz transformation; rapidities add or the 4-momentum), and a measurement settles it, with the historical evidence (Fizeau 1851; Bucherer 1908 and today's accelerators). A Beginner prediction asks how fast the probe goes at a ship speed of 0.80c (wrong options: the speeds add; it is "held" at c), and an Intermediate one asks what doubling the speed from 0.45c to 0.90c does to momentum (wrong options: it doubles; it grows less, because speed is limited). An Advanced synthesis question requires Momentum and collisions: a sticky collision at 0.80c, where conserving γmv and energy gives 0.50c, not the carts' 0.40c, and a merged mass above 2m; its numbers are checked against the physics engine. The practice graphs already on this step are now backed by the experiment, and both topics are covered.
- **A Hubble diagram in Evolving space.** Learners observe nearby galaxies eight at a time, up to 32, and see their recession speeds plotted against distance, with the best straight line through the origin and its slope, H₀. The galaxies are illustrative, generated from the model's H₀ with realistic scatter (peculiar motions of 300 km/s and 10 % distance errors), and the page says so; they are not a real catalogue. A Beginner prediction asks what the diagram looks like (wrong options: an exact line; no pattern; a curve that levels off), and an Intermediate one asks how the estimate changes with more galaxies (wrong options: two galaxies fix it; it keeps rising). With 8 galaxies the estimate is 73.5 ± 2.4 km/s/Mpc, more than two uncertainties from the model's 67.7; with 32 it is 68.4 ± 1.2. The sample and the fit (`src/physics/observationalCosmology.ts`) are tested: no scatter gives H₀ exactly, a large sample recovers it apart from the small, documented bias distance errors cause, and the uncertainty shrinks roughly as 1/√N. Parameter inference from real observations stays a planned topic.
- **Scientific validation, linked and sourced.** `docs/product/SCIENTIFIC_VALIDATION.md` sets the checklist for changing a physics model; `CLAUDE.md` and the pull-request template now point to it. Every empirical value in the engine, and every measurement quoted in learner-facing evidence, has its source and uncertainty next to it and in the policy's table. Numbers a learner reads follow the language (0,93 in Indonesian), enforced by a test.
- **Three kinds of number, each tested.** Text a learner reads follows the language; link parameters are plain data (`queryNumber()`); TeX keeps English digits (`texNumber()`). A test renders every experiment and checks, in every language, that its share link reads back to the same setup and that its maths has no decimal commas. A first version of the language-aware charts sent display formatting into links, so a shared or reloaded "Gravity and clock rates" page lost every clock height of 1 000 m or more, in every language; it was fixed this way. The pull-request checklist now also requires its four standard testing lines, ticked or explained.
- **Localized page titles.** Every page's browser title follows the selected language: experiments use their catalogue title, other pages a translated message.
